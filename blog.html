PRODUCT THINKING

Start with the problem, not the feature list

A product idea often arrives as a set of features. Before designing screens, ask who has the problem, when it happens, and what a better outcome would look like. A sharp problem statement helps teams decide what belongs in the first version—and what can wait.

For a small team, that focus matters. It keeps the work tied to a real need and makes it easier to learn from the first people who use the product.

BUILDING FOR THE WEB

A company website should help people take the next step

A good company website does more than look polished. It explains what the company does, helps visitors find relevant work, and makes the next action clear. That may be reading a product guide, exploring a service, or starting a conversation.

Clear structure, readable content, and responsive design are part of the product—not decoration added at the end.

DIGITAL WELLNESS

Personal tracking should support people, not judge them

Trackers can help people notice routines and reflect on progress. They should leave room for different goals, different days, and the user’s own judgment. A record is useful when it helps someone make a choice; it should not pretend to make that choice for them.

This is one of the ideas behind DIGITracker’s planning, movement, and wellness features.
